Day 16 found a basis for the column space from the pivot columns, and Day 17 found a basis for the null space from the free variables. Those two spaces answer the two questions about $A\mathbf x = \mathbf b$: which right sides have a solution, and how many solutions each one has. Today asks how their sizes are related, and the answer is a count. Every column of a matrix is either a pivot column or a free column, so the two counts always add up to the number of columns. That lets you learn about one space from the other, often without solving anything.

## Rank and nullity

<Definition term="Rank and nullity">
The **rank** of a matrix $A$ is the dimension of its column space, and the **nullity** of $A$ is the dimension of its null space:

$$
\operatorname{rank}A = \dim\operatorname{Col}A, \qquad \operatorname{nullity}A = \dim\operatorname{Nul}A.
$$

The pivot columns of $A$ are a basis for $\operatorname{Col}A$, so the rank is the number of pivot columns. The null space has one basis vector per free variable, so the nullity is the number of free columns.
</Definition>

<Pair>
<Figure src="squash" alt="A tilted teal hexagon, the plane Col A, holds the flattened image of a cube of blue grid lines, with the faint original cube still visible behind it. A pink dashed line labelled Nul A runs through the origin, which glows orange." />

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} \green{1} & \red{0} & \pink{-1} \\ \green{0} & \red{1} & \pink{1} \\ \green{1} & \red{1} & \pink{0} \end{bmatrix}
$$

This is Day 16's matrix, which sends $\mathbb R^3$ to $\mathbb R^3$. Every output lands on the plane $\operatorname{Col}A$, so $\operatorname{rank}A = \teal{2}$. The whole line through $(1, -1, 1)$ lands on $\mathbf 0$, so $\operatorname{nullity}A = \pink{1}$. Two input dimensions survive, one is crushed, and $2 + 1 = 3$.
</Pair>

## The column tally

<Definition term="The Rank Theorem">
If $A$ has $n$ columns, then

$$
\operatorname{rank}A + \operatorname{nullity}A = n.
$$
</Definition>

The proof is a count, and row reduction is only there to make the pivots visible, so an echelon form is enough and the reduced form is not needed. Row reduce $A$ to an echelon form. A column with a pivot adds one vector to the basis of $\operatorname{Col}A$. A column without a pivot gives a free variable, and each free variable adds one vector to the basis of $\operatorname{Nul}A$. No column can be both or neither, so the two counts add up to $n$. Only the number of free columns matters for the null space, not the entries in those columns.

<Pair>
<Figure src="tally" alt="The echelon form with rows (1, 0, 2, 0, 1), (0, 1, −1, 0, 2), (0, 0, 0, 1, −1) and a zero row. Columns 1, 2 and 4 are teal with orange boxes on their pivots, columns 3 and 5 are pink, and x3 and x5 above them are pink. A bar of three teal cells and two pink cells is braced as 3 and 2 above and n = 5 below." />

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 & 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 5 & 1 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \\[4pt]
&\sim \begin{bmatrix} \teal{1} & \teal{0} & \pink{2} & \teal{0} & \pink{1} \\ \teal{0} & \teal{1} & \pink{-1} & \teal{0} & \pink{2} \\ \teal{0} & \teal{0} & \pink{0} & \teal{1} & \pink{-1} \\ \teal{0} & \teal{0} & \pink{0} & \teal{0} & \pink{0} \end{bmatrix} \\[4pt]
\operatorname{rank}A + \operatorname{nullity}A &= \teal{3} + \pink{2} = 5
\end{aligned}
$$
</Pair>

The theorem also answers questions about a matrix you never see. A $7 \times 9$ matrix with a two-dimensional null space has rank $9 - 2 = 7$. Be careful to subtract from the number of **columns**, not rows. Day 19 uses this equation for square matrices to show that crushing nothing and reaching every output are the same condition, and Day 24 restates it for any linear map.

## Row rank equals column rank

Each pivot sits in its own column and also in its own row. The nonzero rows of an echelon form are a basis for $\operatorname{Row}A$, and there is exactly one nonzero row per pivot. So the row space and the column space have the same dimension, even though the rows of a $4 \times 5$ matrix live in $\mathbb R^5$ and its columns live in $\mathbb R^4$.

<Pair>
<Figure src="rows-and-columns" alt="The same echelon form with teal pivot columns and pink free columns. Orange boxes surround the three nonzero rows. Below it, dim Row A = 3 = dim Col A." />

$$
\dim\operatorname{Row}A = \dim\operatorname{Col}A = \operatorname{rank}A
$$

The rows of $A$ are the columns of $A^T$, so this also says $\operatorname{rank}A^T = \operatorname{rank}A$.
</Pair>

## Limits from the shape

An $m \times n$ matrix has at most one pivot in each row and at most one in each column, so $\operatorname{rank}A \le \min(m, n)$. The Rank Theorem then turns that bound into a floor for the nullity: $\operatorname{nullity}A \ge n - m$. A wide matrix, with more columns than rows, always crushes something.

<Figure src="shapes" alt="Left: a 3 by 3 echelon matrix with pivots on the diagonal, three teal cells below it, and 3 + 0 = 3. Right: a 2 by 4 echelon matrix with pivots in columns 1 and 3, cells teal, pink, teal, pink, and 2 + 2 = 4." />

<PivotBudget rows={4} columns={6} />

This is why a $6 \times 9$ matrix cannot have a two-dimensional null space. Its rank would have to be $9 - 2 = 7$, but six rows allow only six pivots.

Day 31 uses the same count to measure eigenspaces, since the dimension of $\operatorname{Nul}(A - \lambda I)$ is $n - \operatorname{rank}(A - \lambda I)$. Tomorrow, on Day 19, this count helps show that a long list of tests for square matrices all decide whether $A$ is invertible.

## Practice

<Check>
A $5 \times 8$ matrix $A$ has rank 3. Find $\operatorname{nullity}A$ and $\dim\operatorname{Row}A$. (Modeled on Lay §4.6, Exercise 5.)

<VectorAnswer answer={[5, 3]} labels={["nullity A", "dim Row A"]} />

<Answer>
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{nullity}A &= n - \operatorname{rank}A = 8 - 3 = 5 \\
\dim\operatorname{Row}A &= \operatorname{rank}A = 3
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation $A\mathbf x = \mathbf b$ is not solvable for every $\mathbf b$ in $\mathbb R^5$, because $\operatorname{Col}A$ is only a 3-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb R^5$.
</Answer>
</Check>

A \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 & 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} \teal{1} & \pink{-2} & \teal{0} & \pink{-1} & \pink{4} \\ \teal{0} & \pink{0} & \teal{1} & \pink{1} & \pink{-1} \\ \teal{0} & \pink{0} & \teal{0} & \pink{0} & \pink{0} \end{bmatrix}

The pivots sit in columns 1 and 3, so $\operatorname{rank}A = 2$. Columns 2, 4 and 5 are free, so $\operatorname{nullity}A = 3$. A basis for $\operatorname{Nul}A$ is $\{(2, 1, 0, 0, 0),\ (1, 0, -1, 1, 0),\ (-4, 0, 1, 0, 1)\}$, one vector per free variable, and $2 + 3 = 5$ columns.
</Answer>
